Customer segmentation ·

Who are the best customers, and who is slipping away?

I scored every customer of a UK online retailer on three things: how recently they last ordered, how often they order and how much they spend. Adding the scores up splits the customer base into four groups, each needing a different marketing approach.

05

A second opinion from K-Means

As a check, I also let K-Means clustering (k = 4) group the customers on their standardised recency, frequency and spend. It agrees on the extremes, Champions and Lost, but cannot separate Loyal from At Risk, so the score-based segments are the more useful ones for marketing.

06

Method

  1. Cleaning: removed rows without a customer ID, returns and cancellations, zero-price rows and duplicates.
  2. Recency, frequency and spend are each scored 1–4 by quartile. Cut-offs: .
  3. The three scores are added (3–12): Champions 10–12, Loyal 7–9, At Risk 4–6, Lost 3.
  4. This calculator gives the same scores as the analysis notebook for every customer; an automated test checks it.
